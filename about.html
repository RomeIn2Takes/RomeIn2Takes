ABOUT THE PROJECT

Rome in Two Takes

Rome in Two Takes was developed as the final project for the Information Modeling and Web Technologies course within the Master's Degree in Digital Humanities and Digital Knowledge at the University of Bologna.

The project explores Rome through cinema, combining film locations, digital itineraries and web design. Six films were selected to represent different historical periods and perspectives on the city, and their locations were organized into two complementary itineraries: Historical Journey and Women & Urban Space.

The overall concept of the website, the selection of the films, the definition of the two itineraries and the base visual identity were developed collaboratively by the whole team.

SHARED WORK

A collaborative project

Some aspects of Rome in Two Takes were developed collectively rather than assigned to a single team member. These include the initial concept, film selection, definition of the two narrative itineraries, information architecture and the development of the website's base visual identity.

VISUAL EXPLORATION

A Journey Through Visual Styles

Beyond its cinematic itineraries, Rome in Two Takes offers an additional way of exploring the relationship between visual culture, history and web design.

Through the timeline at the top of each page, users can switch between seven different graphic styles, each inspired by the visual conventions of a specific historical period.

From early printed aesthetics to contemporary and speculative digital interfaces, these themes reinterpret the same content through different combinations of typography, colour, layout and decorative elements. Particular attention was given to cinematic references, connecting the website's visual identity to its central subject.

While the content and overall structure remain unchanged, each theme offers a different visual experience, highlighting how design influences the perception and presentation of information.
