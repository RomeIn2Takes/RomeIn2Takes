Impressionism & Pointillism

Visual references

We began with one idea: the 1850s theme should evoke the atmosphere of a nineteenth-century artist's notebook and plein-air painting. We used Pinterest to look for references for pointillist textures, subtle brushstroke gradients, impressionist color palettes, sage green accents, and organic, asymmetrical layouts. Those references helped us decide which elements could become part of the site's title, itinerary cards, and image viewer.

01. The Impressionist Vision and Plein-Air Art

The 1850s are the years of artistic transformation, plein-air painting, and the development of pointillist light. The theme does not reconstruct a specific gallery. It evokes the atmosphere where the experience began: the artist's notebook, with its soft textures, organic layouts, and delicate color harmonies.

  1. Mid-19th Century

    A canvas without rigid lines

    Following the evolution of artistic techniques in the nineteenth century, creators embraced the natural outdoors. The shift toward capturing transient light made painting on location a defining choice for modern artists.

    University of Bologna →
  2. 1884–1886

    The Pointillist Technique

    Georges Seurat completed A Sunday on La Grande Jatte, establishing the scientific application of small touches of pure color. In the following years, art critics and audiences made it a cornerstone of Neo-Impressionism.

    Digital Humanities →
  3. 1850–1900

    Art in everyday life

    The cultural movement of the era studies the place of light and nature in daily perceptions, transforming how everyday scenes are observed and represented.

    University of Bologna →
  4. The aesthetic

    The organic layout

    An interface announced its structure through organic containers and asymmetrical curves, where visual elements are placed brushstroke by brushstroke. Organic shapes are described as fluid and harmonious elements that soften digital layouts, so the framing was already part of the art.

02. Before the journey begins

Art

The brushstroke title

The word “Art” sits above the title in Cinzel, with elegant letter-spacing. Layered box-shadow values build the soft golden glow, and the background plate features a fine pointillist texture. A smooth brushstroke animation reveals the title gently.

The frame of color dots

The title is framed by an organic border with color dots scattered along the top and bottom and sides. Each dot is drawn in CSS: a soft chromatic touch, glass-like texture, and a luminous highlight. Two groups of pastel tones alternate, so the visual light seems to shimmer across the frame.

The impressionist canvas

The title appears as elegant lettering on a warm ivory panel lit from the front, with a soft sage-green border and thin brush lines under each row, like an artist's notebook. A small red-clay “Exhibition” line sits above it. When the page loads the panel gently blooms into view, as if the first morning light had just struck the canvas.

04. Colour and materials

  • Warm Ivory #f2ecd9 Main background, invoking vintage paper and canvas surfaces
  • Olive Green #808143 Header styling and structural containers
  • Sage Blue #7b9ea8 Borders, cards, and interactive outlines
  • Terracotta / Rust #d97757 Accent highlights, hover states, and active elements
  • Parchment White #fffefb Card backgrounds and content containers
  • Charcoal Ink #2c2825 Typography and core text color

The page emulates an impressionist palette. Soft ivories and parchment whites create the base canvas, while sage greens, olive accents, and terracotta hues provide harmonious earthy contrasts reminiscent of Seurat's outdoor studies.

09. Itinerary cards and scene viewer

On the homepage, the two itineraries are introduced through cards featuring soft borders and delicate labels. Inside each route, the scene viewer employs organic containers and pointillist background textures, marrying historical exploration with impressionist visual aesthetics.

13. Buttons and interaction states

Buttons feature organic brushstroke-inspired border-radii and smooth gradients. They adapt fluidly to hover and active states, providing tactile visual feedback aligned with an artist's workspace.

14. Animation and responsive behaviour

Transitions are subtle and fluid, echoing organic movements rather than mechanical industrial effects. Layouts adapt cleanly across mobile and desktop screens to preserve readability and artistic grace.
