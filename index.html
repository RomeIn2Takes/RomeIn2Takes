Rome in Two Takes

Discover Rome through cinema

Rome is more than a backdrop for cinema. Across different decades, its streets, neighborhoods, monuments and domestic spaces have reflected political changes, social transformations and individual experiences.

Rome in Two Takes offers two itineraries through real film locations: one follows the historical evolution of twentieth-century Rome, while the other explores the relationship between women, emancipation and urban space.

The films behind our itineraries

Our itineraries are built around six films that portray Rome across different historical periods, social contexts and perspectives. Together, they offer different ways of reading the city: from Fascism and the post-war period to contemporary Rome, and from domestic spaces to the progressive occupation of public space.

Rome, Open City

Roberto Rossellini (1945)

A landmark of Italian Neorealism, the film portrays Rome during the Nazi occupation and the struggle of the Resistance.

Bellissima

Luchino Visconti (1951)

Through Maddalena and her daughter, the film explores working-class Rome and the dreams created by the world of Cinecittà.

Mamma Roma

Pier Paolo Pasolini (1962)

The film follows a mother searching for social redemption within the rapidly transforming outskirts of post-war Rome.

A Special Day

Ettore Scola (1977)

Set during Hitler's visit to Rome in 1938, the film explores Fascism through the intimate meeting of two neighbours.

The Great Beauty

Paolo Sorrentino (2013)

Jep Gambardella moves through a spectacular yet disillusioned Rome, suspended between historical beauty and contemporary emptiness.

There's Still Tomorrow

Paola Cortellesi (2023)

Set in post-war Rome, the film follows Delia's gradual movement towards independence and a different future.
